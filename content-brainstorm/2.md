# 2 — Libertarian Perspectives on the US Health Insurance Crisis

Backlog: [2 — Examine libertarian explanations and reforms](../CONTENT_BACKLOG.md#ppe)

## Aim

Explain the libertarian diagnosis that US health coverage often behaves like prepaid care rather than insurance, and assess the policy distortions alleged on both the demand and supply sides.

## Topic map

- Demand side: employer tax exclusion and job-tied coverage; guaranteed issue and community rating; third-party payment, price opacity, and cost sharing.
- Supply side: certificate-of-need rules; scope-of-practice limits; FDA review and access to drugs and devices.
- Public programs: Medicare and Medicaid payment rules, incentives, and proposed reforms.
- Hard cases and objections: people who become ill before obtaining coverage, chronic conditions, emergencies, high-value care forgone under deductibles, and access under proposed alternatives.

## Research and framing

Treat the supplied voice-discussion summary as a preliminary argument map. Verify historical, legal, and empirical claims; distinguish evidence from the libertarian interpretation; and represent serious opposing perspectives. For each reform, explain expected benefits, distributional effects, and failure modes. Trace interactions between reforms instead of assuming each can be evaluated in isolation.

## Open decisions

- Broad overview or a focused piece on the most upstream reforms, such as policy ownership and portability?
- How much space should go to competing frameworks and evidence disputes?
- Should the piece compare incremental reforms with a more comprehensive market-oriented alternative?
