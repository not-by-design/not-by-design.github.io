# 3 — Rank Tracking vs. Absolute Welfare

Backlog: [3 — Distinguish absolute welfare from relative rank](../CONTENT_BACKLOG.md#ppe)

## Core question

How should public policy distinguish improving the material welfare of the least well off from changing relative income or wealth positions—and what role does ordinary status comparison play in explaining why people care about rank?

## Argument to examine

The working notes propose treating absolute welfare and relative standing as separate goods. They suggest prioritizing real income or wealth at the bottom, then considering rank-focused interventions when they do not harm that priority. The essay should make clear that this is a proposed normative ordering, not a claim that people empirically care only about absolute outcomes.

## Research and objections

- Review work on positional goods, relative income, social comparison, happiness, status, and rank-related health outcomes.
- Distinguish neutral “rank tracking” from envy, while examining whether the terminology itself frames the debate.
- Address revealed preferences, feedback from low rank to health and welfare, and the relationship to Rawls's difference principle.
- Separate psychological explanations for inequality concern from the normative question of whether that concern is justified.
- Test whether first- and second-order effects, including liberty and material outcomes, give the proposed priority rule practical content.

## Open decisions

- Is the main contribution a conceptual distinction, a policy rule, or an empirical review of rank sensitivity?
- Which measure of absolute welfare and which population should anchor the argument?
- What cases best show when rank concerns can or cannot be separated from material welfare?
