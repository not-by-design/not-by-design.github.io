# 1 — The Panic of 1873 in Comparative Perspective

Backlog: [1 — Compare interpretations of the Panic of 1873](../CONTENT_BACKLOG.md#ppe)

## Working question

How much of the US contraction dated 1873–79 was real output weakness, and how much was monetary deflation associated with the return to gold? Compare the episode with the 1920–21 depression and the Panic of 1893 to identify what evidence can distinguish competing interpretations.

## Candidate interpretations

- A monetary reading: the episode was chiefly a price-level adjustment around resumption, with output growing through much of the period.
- A credit-boom reading: the railroad and credit expansion left a substantial misallocation that was liquidated after the crash.
- A data-limits conclusion: available historical series cannot discriminate cleanly, but the rival readings make different predictions that can be stated explicitly.

Treat these as hypotheses to evaluate, not conclusions. The supplied scoping notes say some recalled figures need verification against primary or revised sources.

## Proposed research path

1. Map the main literature and record each author's severity estimates, series, and methods.
2. Assemble comparable event-time data for prices, output or industrial production, money, rates, bank failures, and railroad investment.
3. Compare 1873 with 1920–21 first, then add 1893 as a same-regime comparison.
4. Use descriptive comparisons before deciding whether narrative policy coding is warranted.
5. State where data revisions, chronology, and aggregate measurement limit inference.
