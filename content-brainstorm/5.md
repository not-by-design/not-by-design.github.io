# 5 — Fiction: Time, Perception, and Coming of Age

Backlog: [5 — Plan a coming-of-age story about perceived time](../CONTENT_BACKLOG.md#arts-letters)

## Aim

Write an original short story about the gap between how time passes and how a person perceives it, and the costs of that gap for self-knowledge and relationships. The supplied brainstorm draws on admired short fiction for craft analysis; the new story should develop its own characters, scenes, language, and structure.

## Craft possibilities

- Compress years or life stages, then let the emotional consequences emerge through selected scenes.
- Externalize a character's difficulty through a concrete object or situation.
- Use a restricted or unreliable perspective that explains away change.
- Let tone shift gradually, or show transformation through a final action rather than a named realization.

These are options to test, not a checklist. The lead technique remains undecided.

## Development path

1. Revisit the source stories and identify the specific craft effects that matter, without imitating their expression or distinctive plots.
2. Write a one-sentence premise and identify the relationship at stake.
3. Test two or three structural lenses with short scene sketches.
4. Choose a form—single scene, compressed span, or journey—based on the premise.
5. Draft a rough scene and assess whether the time theme appears in action rather than explanation.

## Open decisions

- Is the protagonist in denial, slowly waking up, or somewhere between?
- What concrete situation can carry the theme without feeling like an imposed symbol?
- What is lost or preserved as time passes?
- Should the ending turn on a quiet gesture or an earned recognition?
