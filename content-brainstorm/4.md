# 4 — Theories of Narrative

Backlog: [4 — Develop a framework of narrative theories](../CONTENT_BACKLOG.md#arts-letters)

## Aim

Develop a framework that describes what narratives do and can also help a writer make deliberate choices. The supplied notes group narrative approaches around transformation and resilience, meaning-making, catharsis and restored order, psychological realism, aesthetic form, and political critique.

## Working structure

For each school, define its central claim about narrative, identify representative thinkers or works, and ask what it predicts about character, conflict, form, and an ending. Then explore how the framework can guide creation without reducing stories to formulas or audience manipulation.

The notes suggest that dramatic restoration can become propaganda when the desired audience effect dictates the story. Examine whether anchoring a work in human transformation or existential meaning addresses that risk, or merely shifts it.

## Research and development

- Verify attributions and concepts, including Aristotle's catharsis, Frankl's will to meaning, McKee's account of story, Brechtian estrangement, and aesthetic formalism.
- Clarify overlaps and disagreements among the categories rather than presenting them as a settled taxonomy.
- Use a small number of literary or screen examples to test the framework.
- Consider whether the output is an essay, a writer's guide, or a framework to be tested in a separate creative project.

## Open decisions

- Is the goal primarily criticism, a practical craft tool, or both?
- Which theories belong in the final framework, and what organizing principle connects them?
- How can the piece preserve plural reasons for storytelling without implying one universal purpose?
