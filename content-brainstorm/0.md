# 0 — Left-Populist Themes

Backlog: [0 — Map left-populist economic themes](../CONTENT_BACKLOG.md#ppe)

## Aim

Organize a broad set of left-populist claims about markets, labor, finance, democratic power, externalities, public goods, and technology into a useful public-facing treatment. The source notes range from concentrated market power and labor exploitation to climate, corporate capture, and precautionary regulation.

## Possible shape

Choose between one survey-style essay that maps the recurring themes and a series of focused posts that examine individual claims. A survey could show the common argument—concentrated private power shifts costs and risks onto workers and the public—while a series would allow closer evidence checks and stronger treatment of counterarguments. A hybrid is also possible: a concise overview linked to later deep dives.

## Research and framing

- Separate empirical claims (for example, profit margins, productivity and pay, wage theft, private-equity outcomes) from normative conclusions and policy proposals.
- Define terms such as “greedflation,” “monopsony,” “financialization,” and “public good” rather than treating them as self-evident.
- Test whether the different themes share one explanatory frame or need distinct arguments.
- Include serious counterarguments and cases where competition, private provision, or market incentives may work well.
- Verify all quantitative and causal claims before publication; the supplied notes are an idea map, not a sourced draft.

## Open decisions

- Survey, series, or overview plus linked deep dives?
- Which themes have enough evidence and a clear argument to lead?
- Is the central subject concentrated corporate power, or the broader left-populist diagnosis of capitalism and public policy?
- What publication length and audience should guide the research?
